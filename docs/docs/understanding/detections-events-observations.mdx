# Detections, events and observations

These three words mean different things. Say one deer triggers your camera 40 times in an afternoon. That is 40 detections, 1 event, and 1 observation with a count of 1.

## Detection

One box around one animal, person or vehicle, in one file. A photo with three deer has three detections. Each detection has a label and a confidence score, and it's what you check on the Labels page.

## Event

Files from the same camera, taken close together in time. If a deer walks past and the camera fires 12 times in one minute, that is one event, not 12.

A new event starts after a gap longer than the independence interval, 30 minutes by default. The gap is measured from one photo to the next, not from the start of the event. So an event can run for hours if the camera keeps triggering.

A deployment with [paired cameras](./how-a-project-is-organised.mdx#paired-cameras) counts as one camera here.

## Observation

One species inside one event, with a count. An event with 8 photos of deer and 2 of a fox has two observations: deer and fox. If each photo shows one animal, both counts are 1, not 8 and 2.

On the Counts page you can split a species into groups, for example adults and juveniles. Each group is then its own observation.

## How the count is worked out

The AI suggests the highest number of that species in any single photo of the event. This is often called MaxN. For example, a deer walks past and the camera takes 5 photos:

| Photo | Deer visible |
|---|---|
| 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 2 |
| 3 | 2 |
| 4 | 1 |
| 5 | 0 |

So the suggestion is 2, not 6. Counting every photo would count the same deer again.

On the Counts page you confirm that number or change it. Your number then replaces the suggestion everywhere: in the charts, the rates and the exports. See [confirm the counts](../guides/confirm-counts.mdx).

## Videos

In a video, the AI follows each animal from frame to frame. So a detection is one animal followed through the video, and it gets one card on the Labels page. What you do with that card applies to every frame of the animal.

The AI's guess can change from frame to frame. One raccoon was read as raccoon, badger, badger, blank and opossum on five frames in a row. That's noise, and the card's label is what counts.

The count for a video is the most animals of a species in any single frame.

## Where each number appears

| Number | Where you see it |
|---|---|
| Detections | Labels page, detections export |
| Events | Counts page, dashboard tile |
| Observations | Dashboard, map, charts, counts export |
